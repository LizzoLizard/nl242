The maximum nonlinearity of a balanced nine-variable Boolean function is exactly $240$. Thus $n = 9$ is the smallest odd dimension in which requiring balance strictly lowers the maximum nonlinearity below the covering radius ($240 < 242 \le \rho(1,9)$, whereas balanced functions attain $\rho(1,n) = 0, 2, 12, 56$ for $n = 1, 3, 5, 7$), resolving a problem open since Kavut, Maitra, and Yücel constructed unbalanced $9$-variable functions of nonlinearity $242$ in 2006--2007.

We develop a spectral-phase plateau theory for putative balanced $9$-variable functions with $\max_a |W_F(a)| \le 28$, combining a bottom-up high-block quartic exclusion pipeline ($t \le 98$) with a top-down low-block $\mathrm{RM}(3,8)$, signed-Delsarte, and four-cell rank-$6$ exclusion pipeline ($t \ge 100$). The structural architecture and upper-endpoint closures ($t = 112, 114, 118$, $t \ge 120$, and $t = 116, k = 12$) are formalized in zero-Mathlib Lean 4.15.0 (79 compiled modules, zero sorry, zero custom axioms), culminating in coordinate-free master theorems. The final $t = 116, k = 16$ endpoint is closed by three independent routes (direct $\mathrm{AG}(6,2)$ enumeration, an independent $\mathrm{RM}(3,5)$ code-lift cross-check, and an independent re-verification of $\rho(1,9) < 244$). All Lean modules, companion proofs, and SHA-256-manifested replay packets are archived at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24447815 and https://github.com/LizzoLizard/nl242.
